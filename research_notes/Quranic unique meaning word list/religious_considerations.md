# Islamic Religious Considerations for a Seed-Phrase Word List Drawn from Quranic Arabic Vocabulary

Scope note: These notes summarise rulings and scholarly views. They do not issue a ruling. Sources are mostly online fatwa services (Hanafi South African and UK muftis, Shafi'i fiqh sites, Saudi scholars via IslamQA, Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, Malaysian state fatwa committees, Islamweb). Where a finding comes only from a search-result snippet and not from a fetched page, it is marked "(snippet)". Research date: October 2026. I found no fatwa anywhere that addresses seed phrases or cryptographic word lists built from Quranic vocabulary. Everything below is reasoned by analogy from rulings on paper, coins, signs, screens and products.

---

## 1. Do mushaf rules (purity to touch, not discarding, not taking into toilets) apply to SINGLE words taken from the Quran that are also ordinary Arabic vocabulary?

### Takeaway
The classical rules attach to text that is written or recited *as Quran*. In the Shafi'i school that means text "written for the purpose of study". In all schools intention (qasd) matters for recitation. Text that is not a mushaf, or Quranic wording used for another purpose (dhikr, du'a, money, clothing), gets lighter treatment. No source treats an isolated, ordinary Arabic word as "Quran" just because it occurs in the Quran. The sanctity follows the Quran's ordered wording (nazm) and the user's intention, not individual dictionary words. Scholars differ on borderline cases such as phone screens, coins and a single verse.

### Cited Findings
- **Shafi'i school, touching and carrying.** *'Umdat al-Salik* (Reliance of the Traveller) forbids touching or carrying "any of the Koran written for the purpose of study, even a single verse" without purity. The same answer allows carrying money, rings or clothing with Quranic inscriptions. It also allows carrying tafsir, hadith or fiqh books with Quranic passages when non-Quranic text predominates, and amulets not written for study. It cites al-Nawawi (*Tibyan*, *Minhaj*, *Majmu'*) and al-Khatib al-Shirbini (*Mughni al-Muhtaj*). — [Shafiifiqh via IslamQA.org](https://islamqa.org/shafii/shafiifiqh/30007/can-one-in-major-ritual-impurity-recite-the-quran-what-about-carry-or-touch-it/)
- **Intention decides whether words count as Quran recitation.** Al-Nawawi's *Minhaj* allows someone in major impurity to say the Quran's adhkar "having the intention of dhikr and not the intention of reciting the Qur'an". The same source reports consensus that "Subhan Allah", "La ilaha illa Allah", "Al-hamdu lillah" and similar phrases are lawful in that state, even though they also occur in the Quran. — [Shafiifiqh via IslamQA.org](https://islamqa.org/shafii/shafiifiqh/30007/can-one-in-major-ritual-impurity-recite-the-quran-what-about-carry-or-touch-it/)
- **Hanafi view on intention.** Someone in janabah may recite verses as a supplication, but not with the intention of reciting the Quran (snippet). — [MuftiOnline via IslamQA.org](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/muftionline/103731/reciting-and-touching-the-quran-in-the-state-of-janaabat/); also [SeekersGuidance (Hanafi)](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hanafi-fiqh/can-those-in-major-impurity-or-menstruation-recite-quranic-verses-for-protection/) (snippet: it is permissible to "supplicate even with verses from the Quran, provided they do not intend them as recitation").
- **Text outside a mushaf is not a mushaf.** Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya (Fatwa 2244, Mufti Ali Gomaa, 4 May 2009) allows verses written outside the mushaf in ordinary modern (imla'i) spelling instead of Uthmani script. Its reason is that such writing "does not qualify as a mushaf" (لا يصدُق عليه وصف المصحف). It cites al-Baqillani (*al-Intisar*) and notes that most scholars and modern fiqh councils limit the Uthmani-script requirement to actual mushafs. — [Dar al-Ifta, Fatwa 12390 page](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/Fatwa/Details/12390)
- **Coins with Quranic text (closest analogy to an everyday object handled by everyone).** Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya (Fatwa 5560, Sheikh Muhammad Khater, 24 Feb 1973) ruled that printing any part of the Quran on circulating currency is *makruh* (disliked): "يكره طباعة شيء من القرآن الكريم على العملات". It gave three reasons: loss of reverence; handling by people in states of impurity; and no necessity. It cites Ibn al-Humam's *Fath al-Qadir* (Hanafi), which forbids writing the Quran and God's names on coins, pulpits and items exposed to defilement. — [Dar al-Ifta, Fatwa 16252 page](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/16252)
- **Schools differ on coins.** The Shafi'i texts above allow carrying money bearing Quranic inscriptions without purity — [Shafiifiqh](https://islamqa.org/shafii/shafiifiqh/30007/can-one-in-major-ritual-impurity-recite-the-quran-what-about-carry-or-touch-it/). The Hanafi-based Egyptian fatwa calls putting Quran on coins disliked — [Dar al-Ifta](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/16252).
- **Phone screens (relevant if words appear on wallet devices or apps). Views differ:**
  - Strict view: wudu is needed to touch the part of the screen showing Quran. When entering the bathroom, the app should be closed so Quran is not on screen; if the text is not visible, the phone may be taken in. — [Askimam via IslamQA.org](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/102917/witr-qadha-is-wudhu-necessary-for-touching-the-phone-while-the-qur-an-app-is-on/)
  - Lenient view: touching is allowed without wudu, because the wudu rule covers "permanent legible written words" and screen pixels are not solid writing (snippet). — [Darul Ifta Birmingham](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/?p=20614)
- **Historical precedent for the gold dinar.** Umayyad caliph Abd al-Malik's reformed all-epigraphic gold coinage was the first use of Quranic verses on coinage by a Muslim ruler (snippet). — [Virtual Museum Palestine](https://virtualmuseum.tourism.ps/en/article/31/Umayyad-Gold-Dinar); al-Walid I's dinars carried "There is no god but Allah, He is One, He has no partner" and a Quranic border inscription (snippet) — [Yale VRME](https://vrme.yale.edu/posts/2024-08-07-dinar-of-al-walid-i). Later jurists (Ibn al-Humam, Dar al-Ifta 1973) disliked the practice for the purity reasons above — [Dar al-Ifta](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/16252).

### Inferences
- A random list of lemma-form words, written without Quranic ordering and not intended as recitation, is unlikely to meet the classical definition of "Quran written for study" or "a mushaf". The cited rules point to intention, nazm, and whether the writing functions as Quran. On that reasoning, purity-to-touch and toilet rules for the mushaf would mostly not apply to a seed backup of ordinary words.
- The stricter Hanafi/Egyptian position on coins and the strict view on phone screens show that some scholars and users will still treat anything presented as "from the Quran" with mushaf-like caution. Calling the list "Quranic words" invites that treatment. "Classical Arabic words (all attested in the Quran)" does not.
- The project explicitly draws on the classical gold dinar. The Abd al-Malik precedent cuts both ways: Quranic text on money is historic, but later jurists judged it disliked. That is a good argument for not putting sacred text on a money-related artifact.

### Gaps
- I found no source that explicitly rules on a *single word* taken in isolation, as opposed to a verse or part of a verse, written outside a Quranic context. The Hanafi debate on reciting "less than a verse" in janabah (al-Karkhi vs. al-Tahawi) was not retrieved in a fetchable primary source.
- I found no fatwa on whether a person may take into a toilet a paper or metal item carrying ordinary Arabic words that also occur in the Quran.

## 2. Names of Allah (al-asma' al-husna), prophets' names and sacred words on everyday items that may be discarded

### Takeaway
Fatwa bodies broadly agree that paper bearing Allah's name, angels' or prophets' names, or Quranic verses must not be thrown in the trash. The approved disposal methods are burying, placing in running water, burning or shredding. Several Hanafi muftis require erasing the divine name before burning or shredding. Scholars distinguish names *exclusive* to Allah (Allah, al-Rahman, al-Khaliq, al-Ahad, al-Samad, al-Rabb, al-Quddus) from names *shared* with creatures (Karim, Rahim, Halim, Hakim, 'Alim, Malik, 'Aziz). Shared names may describe people and things. However, the disposal fatwas generally do not exempt shared-name words. One Hanafi mufti treats even "Assalamu alaykum" as needing respectful disposal, because al-Salam is a divine name.

### Cited Findings
- **Askimam (Mufti Ebrahim Desai, South Africa).**
  - Material with Quranic content should be buried or placed in running water. Burning is "permitted though not preferable".
  - Words like "Allah", "Jazakallah" and "Assalamu alaykum" ("Salaam" being a divine name) should be removed before disposal, or the whole item disposed of by these methods.
  - Cites *Fatawa Mahmudiyya* vol. 1 p. 17.
  - Source: [IslamQA.org/Askimam](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/11704/is-it-permissible-to-throw-away-papers-with-the-name-of-allah-on-them-or-not-what-is-the-proper-way-to-dispose-of/)
- **MuftiSays (Hanafi).**
  - Papers with Allah's name may be burned "only after erasing the name of Allah Ta'ala, His Angels and Messengers". It is best to bury them, and casting them into a flowing river is acceptable.
  - Actual Quran pages may not be burned; they should be weighted and cast into a river, or buried.
  - Source: [IslamQA.org/MuftiSays](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/muftisays/10078/how-to-dispose-of-leaflets-and-papers-with-allah-s-name-and-quranic-ayats/)
- **Shredding (Hanafi, Shaykh Muhammad ibn Adam al-Kawthari).**
  - Shredding is "akin to burning", so it is permitted after erasing the names of Allah, His angels and His messengers.
  - The remains should not go in the bin but be buried where people do not walk.
  - Source: [IslamQA.org/Darul Iftaa](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa/8512/using-a-paper-shredder-to-dispose-of-material-with-quranic-verses-on-them/)
- **Newspapers (Askimam).** Newspapers containing Allah's name or verses should be wrapped in cloth and buried in ground rarely walked on. In cities where burial is hard, burning is an alternative (snippet). — [IslamQA.org/Askimam](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/1328/newspapers-have-a-lot-of-names-of-allah-and-rasul-s-a-w-especially-urdu-newspapers-can-one-trash-the-newspaper/)
- **Egypt, Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya (English fatwa, 20 Aug 2013).**
  - Intentional desecration of Allah's names or verses is apostasy; careless disposal is a serious wrong but less grave.
  - It recommends avoiding papers bearing Allah's names where possible, and otherwise "burning or recycling them" or cutting them "into small pieces".
  - It urges authorities to set up collection systems.
  - It makes no distinction for names shared with people.
  - Source: [Dar al-Ifta Egypt, Fatwa 6306](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6306/throwing-papers-containing-quranic-verses-and-the-beautiful-names-of-allah-in-tr)
- **Saudi Arabia, Permanent Committee (al-Lajna al-Da'ima) and Ibn Uthaymin, via IslamQA.**
  - Ibn Uthaymin: papers with verses or hadith should not be used for food or sat on.
  - The Committee: pick up such papers "and burn it or bury it in clean ground that is not where people walk", but "You are not obliged to do that which causes hardship for yourself".
  - Source: [IslamQA 158570](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/158570)
- **Saudi Arabia, Ibn Baz.** Writing a name such as "Abd al-Qadir" on documents is fine. If the papers are later thrown away, "the sin falls on whoever does this, but the writer bears no sin" (الإثم على مَنْ فعل ذلك، أمّا الكاتب فليس عليه إثم). — [IslamQA (Arabic) 39376](https://islamqa.info/ar/ref/39376)
- **Al-Nawawi, *al-Tibyan*.** "It is disliked to inscribe the Quran and the Beautiful Names of Allah on walls and garments" (snippet). — [IslamQA 264601](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/264601)
- **Islamweb (27 Dec 2017).**
  - "It is not permissible to write anything from the Quran on something that might be treated with disrespect."
  - Probable disrespect (walls, curtains) is disliked; certain disrespect (carpets, mats) is forbidden.
  - Disposal: preserve, burn, scrape off the inscription, or bury in a clean place. Cites 'Uthman's burning of variant copies as honourable.
  - Relief if disposal is genuinely difficult.
  - Source: [Islamweb 367540](https://islamweb.net/en/printfatwa.php?id=367540)
- **Disagreement on burning.** MuftiSays says Quran pages must not be burned — [link](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/muftisays/10078/how-to-dispose-of-leaflets-and-papers-with-allah-s-name-and-quranic-ayats/). Askimam says burning is allowed but not preferred — [link](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/11704/is-it-permissible-to-throw-away-papers-with-the-name-of-allah-on-them-or-not-what-is-the-proper-way-to-dispose-of/). Islamweb and Dar al-Ifta Egypt present burning as acceptable — [Islamweb](https://islamweb.net/en/printfatwa.php?id=367540); [Dar al-Ifta](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6306/throwing-papers-containing-quranic-verses-and-the-beautiful-names-of-allah-in-tr).
- **Exclusive vs. shared divine names.**
  - Exclusive names include Allah, al-Rahman, al-Rabb and al-Ahad. Using them for anything else "is like competing or disputing with Allah".
  - Shared names (Halim, Karim, Rahim, Hakim) may describe people and things. Ibn al-Qayyim is cited: "it is permissible to call people and things by names of Allah... which are not unique to Him".
  - Calling a dish "haleem" is ruled permissible.
  - Source: [IslamQA 314410](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/314410)
- **Further lists of exclusive and shared names.** Exclusive names include al-Khaliq, al-Bari', al-Razzaq, al-Samad and al-Quddus. Al-Malik and al-'Aziz may be used for others (snippet). — [Islamweb 88517](https://islamweb.net/en/fatwa/88517/using-allaahs-names); [al-Feqh: Calling creatures with the Creator's Names](https://www.al-feqh.com/en/calling-creatures-with-the-creator-s-names)

### Inferences
- **The cautious design choice is to exclude:**
  - The word "Allah" and all exclusive divine names (al-Rahman, al-Khaliq, al-Bari', al-Razzaq, al-Quddus, al-Samad, al-Ahad, al-Rabb, etc.).
  - Words built on a divine name ("salam" as a noun, "ilah"/"rabb").
  - Angels' names (Jibril, Mika'il) and prophets' names (Muhammad, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa, etc.). Hanafi disposal fatwas explicitly group "Allah, His angels and His messengers".
- **Shared-name adjectives** (karim, rahim, 'aziz, hakim, 'alim, halim, malik) are ordinary vocabulary by the sources above. A respectful list would still avoid them: on a random backup they would read as divine names, and disposal fatwas do not exempt them.
- **A verbs-only list** avoids most names, since names are nouns or adjectives, but see Section 6.

### Gaps
- No source found states whether shared-name words used in a *non-divine* sense on discarded paper carry the same disposal duty. The disposal fatwas are silent on the distinction.
- No source found on metal backups specifically (stamping, melting, grinding). The closest is Islamweb's "scrape away inscriptions" option.

## 3. Random juxtaposition, rearranging Quranic words (tahrif/tankis), and commercial use of Quranic text

### Takeaway
- **Reordering Quranic words is prohibited when it is done to Quranic speech**, as in recitation. The reason given is that it takes God's speech out of the form in which He spoke it. Verse order is held to be divinely fixed (tawqifi).
- **Commercial use of Quranic verses to promote a product** is prohibited in gazetted fatwas from several Malaysian states.

I found no ruling on ordinary words that happen to occur in the Quran being arranged randomly. The risk lies in two places: random sequences that *look like* or *are presented as* Quran, and branding a product as "Quranic" for commercial appeal.

### Cited Findings
- **Reversing letters or words of the Quran (tankis) is forbidden.** Reading "أحد الله هو قل" for "قل هو الله أحد" constitutes "إخراج لكلام الله عن الوجه الذي تكلم الله به" (taking God's speech out of the form He spoke it). Reversing letters (e.g., ربّ to برّ) invalidates prayer. Verse order is "توقيف من الله تعالى" (citing Ibn Uthaymin). Changing the order of surahs is allowed on the stronger view. — [IslamOnline Fiqh: al-Tankis fi qira'at al-Qur'an](https://fiqh.islamonline.net/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%83%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%A1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A2%D9%86/)
- **Quoting part of a verse is fine.** Dar al-Ifta Egypt notes that the long practice of reciters, teachers and authors of quoting part of a verse, or stopping on a word in the middle of one, is unobjectionable "unless it leads to something prohibited" (snippet). — [Dar al-Ifta Egypt, Fatwa 12774](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/12774)
- **Malaysia, Federal Territories.**
  - The Federal Territory Syarak Law Consultative Committee (105th meeting, 30 Nov 2017) prohibits "the use of verses of the Quran for the purpose of promoting a product". The fatwa was issued 12 July 2021 and gazetted 19 July 2021 as P.U.(B) 369/2021.
  - Its basis is *sadd al-dhara'i* (blocking means to wrongdoing), to prevent "the sale of the religion in return for worldly gains". General use of verses remains permitted.
  - Similar fatwas exist in Perlis, Negeri Sembilan and Penang.
  - Source: [Skrine legal alert](https://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/july-2021/federal-territories-fatwa-sale-of-products-using-v)
- **Malaysia, Perlis.** The Perlis State Fatwa Committee prohibits using ruqyah (Quranic) verses on any product for commercial purposes, "whether for labelling, advertising, or promotional purposes" (snippet). — [Mufti Perlis](https://muftiperlis.gov.my/index.php/fatwa/49-muamalah/1389-ruling-on-the-sale-of-products-using-ruqyah-verses-verses-of-al-qur-an-for-commercial-purposes)
- **Islamweb on objects.** Writing Quran on objects likely to be treated with disrespect is impermissible. — [Islamweb 367540](https://islamweb.net/en/printfatwa.php?id=367540)

### Inferences
- The tankis prohibition is about reciting or presenting the Quran in altered order. A random sequence of lemma words is not offered as Quran and does not reproduce verse order, so it is not tankis in the technical sense. Two risks remain:
  - **(a) Accidental reproduction of Quranic collocations.** For example, a verb plus noun that forms a recognisable phrase from a verse, or a sequence beginning with "qul" (say!) followed by other Quranic words. This could look like a garbled or fabricated verse.
  - **(b) Offensive pairings.** Words for punishment, curse, disbelief or hellfire next to words referring to God or prophets.
- Mitigations for both: exclude divine and prophetic names, exclude eschatological and punishment vocabulary, avoid distinctive Quranic imperatives and formulae (e.g., "qul", "bismi", "subhana"), and present words in dictionary (lemma) form, not verbatim inflected Quranic forms.
- The Malaysian fatwas target *Quranic verses* used to promote products. Isolated vocabulary is not verses. Still, marketing the wallet as using "Quranic words" for religious appeal could be read as the "selling religion for worldly gain" concern those fatwas target, especially in Malaysia. Neutral naming ("a Classical Arabic word list; every word is attested in the Quran") reduces that risk.

### Gaps
- No fatwa found on games, puzzles, crosswords or word lists built from Quranic vocabulary (an Arabic search returned only the general partial-verse ruling).
- No fatwa found on software identifiers, passwords or cryptographic keys made from Quranic words.

## 4. Precedents: Islamic fintech/crypto projects, Shariah boards, and official bodies

### Takeaway
Shariah reviews of crypto and gold-token projects (Amanie Advisors for HelloGold's GOLDX; the 2022 Islamic Coin/HAQQ fatwa) looked at *financial* questions: asset backing, gharar, riba, settlement timing, the consensus mechanism and charity. I found no evidence that any Shariah board has reviewed seed-phrase vocabulary or UI language. The closest technical precedent, an Arabic BIP39 wordlist proposal in 2025, was closed for interoperability reasons and had no religious discussion. Official bodies that address "Quran on everyday items" (Dar al-Ifta Egypt, the Saudi Permanent Committee, Malaysian state fatwa committees) do so through the coin, paper, signage and product rulings above.

### Cited Findings
- **HelloGold GOLDX (Malaysia).**
  - Amanie Advisors certified HelloGold's GOLDX token, launched end of 2017, as backed by physical gold stored in a Singapore vault.
  - The review covered "transparency, certainty and immediacy of transactions" and required "transactions must be completed within a defined time period".
  - HelloGold's gold-trading app was also Shariah-certified by Amanie.
  - This gold-referenced precedent is directly comparable to Bucks.
  - Source: [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/your-money%2Fcryptocurrency%2Fmalaysian-firm-adds-islamic-certification-to-cryptocurrency-1.2176424)
- **Islamic Coin / HAQQ.** Islamic Coin (ISLM, HAQQ Network) reportedly obtained a fatwa in June 2022 led by Sheikh Dr Nizam Yaquby. It was based on proof-of-stake, charity commitments and decentralisation, and the project has a "Shariah Oracle" that marks smart contracts as compliant (snippets from secondary news; not verified against a primary fatwa text). — [Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2023-05-12/islamic-coin-first-sharia-compliant-cryptocurrency-set-launch-may); [Coinranking](https://coinranking.com/coin/CTwRCEBz3+islamiccoin-islm/about)
- **Mufti Faraz Adam (Amanah Advisors).** He has published Hanafi-based research on crypto's status as money (mal, taqawwum, thamaniyya) (snippet). — [GDF interview](https://www.gdf.io/resources/interview-with-faraz-adam-executive-director-at-amanah-advisors/); [Darul Iftaa Birmingham via IslamQA.org](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa-birmingham/171369/is-there-anything-that-makes-crypto-currency-haram/)
- **BIP39 Arabic PR #1925 (10 Aug 2025).**
  - It proposed an Arabic BIP39 wordlist. The PR was closed because BIP39 authors "no longer support further wordlists except for the English one, in order to maximize interoperability".
  - Reviewers noted the words were stored reversed (an RTL handling bug), that it was unclear whether the list used Modern Standard Arabic or a dialect, and that there are "very few (if any) widely used Arabic Bitcoin wallets".
  - The thread contained no religious or Quranic discussion.
  - Source: [BIP39 PR #1925 mirror](https://mirror.b10c.me/bitcoin-bips/1925/)
- **Official bodies on Quran or divine names on everyday items:**
  - Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya: coins (1973, makruh) — [16252](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/16252); paper disposal (2013) — [6306](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6306/throwing-papers-containing-quranic-verses-and-the-beautiful-names-of-allah-in-tr); verses outside the mushaf (2009) — [12390](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/Fatwa/Details/12390)
  - Saudi Permanent Committee, via IslamQA — [158570](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/158570)
  - Ibn Baz — [39376](https://islamqa.info/ar/ref/39376)
  - Malaysian Federal Territories (2021) — [Skrine](https://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/july-2021/federal-territories-fatwa-sale-of-products-using-v); Perlis — [Mufti Perlis](https://muftiperlis.gov.my/index.php/fatwa/49-muamalah/1389-ruling-on-the-sale-of-products-using-ruqyah-verses-verses-of-al-qur-an-for-commercial-purposes)
  - Deobandi-affiliated Hanafi muftis (Askimam, Darul Iftaa/al-Kawthari) — [11704](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/11704/is-it-permissible-to-throw-away-papers-with-the-name-of-allah-on-them-or-not-what-is-the-proper-way-to-dispose-of/), [8512](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa/8512/using-a-paper-shredder-to-dispose-of-material-with-quranic-verses-on-them/)

### Inferences
- A Shariah certification for Bucks would normally cover the gold reference, issuance, custody, riba and gharar. If the owner wants the *word list* reviewed, the list and the user-facing disposal guidance must be explicitly included in the review scope. There is no standard practice to rely on.
- Amanie Advisors (gold token) and Mufti Faraz Adam / Amanah Advisors (crypto) have relevant track records and could be approached. A Dar al-Ifta or national fatwa-body opinion on the specific word-list question would be novel.

### Gaps
- No AAOIFI Shariah Standard was found that addresses religious vocabulary in product design or UI. I did not retrieve any AAOIFI digital-asset standard text in this session.
- No JAKIM, Darul Uloom Deoband (central fatwa department) or Fiqh Council of North America fatwa was found that addresses Quranic words on everyday items or software specifically.
- No Islamic wallet or app was found that uses Quranic vocabulary for keys or passwords.

## 5. Practical mitigations

### Takeaway
The sources support a layered approach:
- Exclude the most sensitive words.
- Use dictionary or lemma forms rather than verbatim Quranic forms.
- Avoid branding the list as "Quranic".
- Tell users how to store and dispose of backups respectfully, using the methods the fatwas endorse (shred and bury, burn, place in running water, or scrape off inscriptions).
- Seek Shariah review that explicitly covers the word list.

Most of the following is inference from the cited rulings, not a ruling.

### Cited Findings (basis for each mitigation)
- **Exclude divine names, angels and prophets.** Hanafi disposal fatwas require erasing "the name of Allah..., His angels and His messengers" before burning or shredding. — [MuftiSays](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/muftisays/10078/how-to-dispose-of-leaflets-and-papers-with-allah-s-name-and-quranic-ayats/); [al-Kawthari](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa/8512/using-a-paper-shredder-to-dispose-of-material-with-quranic-verses-on-them/)
- **Exclude greeting and formula words containing divine names**, e.g. "salam": Askimam treats "Assalamu alaykum" as containing a divine name. — [Askimam 11704](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/11704/is-it-permissible-to-throw-away-papers-with-the-name-of-allah-on-them-or-not-what-is-the-proper-way-to-dispose-of/)
- **Use non-mushaf orthography (lemma, imla'i spelling).** Text outside the mushaf in ordinary spelling is permitted and "does not qualify as a mushaf". — [Dar al-Ifta 12390](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/Fatwa/Details/12390)
- **Avoid Quranic verse fragments and reordered verses.** See the tankis ruling. — [IslamOnline Fiqh](https://fiqh.islamonline.net/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%86%D9%83%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%A1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A2%D9%86/)
- **Avoid marketing "Quranic" content to sell a product.** — [Federal Territories fatwa (Skrine)](https://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/july-2021/federal-territories-fatwa-sale-of-products-using-v)
- **Disposal guidance.** Burying or running water is preferred and burning is allowed (Askimam). Shredding is allowed after erasing names, with remains buried (al-Kawthari). Cutting into small pieces, burning or recycling (Dar al-Ifta Egypt). Burn, scrape off, or bury in a clean place (Islamweb). — [Askimam](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/11704/is-it-permissible-to-throw-away-papers-with-the-name-of-allah-on-them-or-not-what-is-the-proper-way-to-dispose-of/); [al-Kawthari](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa/8512/using-a-paper-shredder-to-dispose-of-material-with-quranic-verses-on-them/); [Dar al-Ifta 6306](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6306/throwing-papers-containing-quranic-verses-and-the-beautiful-names-of-allah-in-tr); [Islamweb 367540](https://islamweb.net/en/printfatwa.php?id=367540)
- **Hardship relief and responsibility.** Users need only do what they reasonably can — [IslamQA 158570](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/158570). The writer bears no sin if others later discard the paper — [Ibn Baz, IslamQA 39376](https://islamqa.info/ar/ref/39376)
- **Device screens.** On the strict view, Quranic text should not be on screen in the bathroom — [Askimam 102917](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/102917/witr-qadha-is-wudhu-necessary-for-touching-the-phone-while-the-qur-an-app-is-on/)

### Inferences (design guidance derived from the above; not rulings)
1. **Exclusion categories** (cautious superset):
   - (a) "Allah", "ilah", "rabb" and all 99 names in every form, exclusive and shared adjectives alike (karim, rahim, 'aziz, hakim, 'alim, halim, malik, salam, haqq, nur, etc.).
   - (b) Names of prophets, angels, the Prophet's family and Companions, and named Quranic persons, peoples and places (Makka, Bakka, Ka'ba, Badr, etc.).
   - (c) Unseen and eschatological terms (janna, nar, jahannam, qiyama, sa'a as "the Hour", 'arsh, kursi, ruh, wahy, malak, shaytan, iblis, jinn).
   - (d) Acts of worship and ritual terms (salat, zakat, sawm, hajj, sujud, ruku', wudu', tawaf, qibla, masjid).
   - (e) Words for revelation and scripture (qur'an, kitab in the scriptural sense, aya, sura, mushaf, tanzil, nabi, rasul).
   - (f) Punishment, curse, disbelief and obscene-adjacent words (la'na, kufr, shirk, 'adhab, munafiq, zina), to prevent offensive juxtapositions.
   - (g) Verbs whose Quranic subject is almost always God and which denote exclusive divine acts: khalaqa (create ex nihilo), bara'a, ahya/amata (give life/death), razaqa (provide), ghafara (forgive sins), anzala (send down revelation), ba'atha (resurrect/send prophets).
   - (h) Distinctive Quranic formulae and imperatives (qul, iqra', bismi, subhana, tabaraka, inna).
2. **Lemma, non-Uthmani form.**
   - Show words in modern standard (imla'i) spelling, dictionary form (e.g., the third-person masculine singular past for verbs), without Quranic waqf marks or Uthmani rasm.
   - Consider omitting harakat or using minimal vowelling, so the backup reads as a vocabulary list, not scripture.
   - This also helps typing and normalisation. The BIP39 Arabic PR shows that RTL and normalisation handling is a real risk.
3. **Naming.** Use something like "Bucks Classical Arabic word list (every word attested in the Quran)" or "Arabic root words". Avoid "Quranic words", "Quran seed" or "holy words" in UI and marketing (Malaysian "selling religion" concern; Egyptian and Hanafi caution about Quran on money).
4. **Collocation screening.** After building the list, test-generate many phrases and check for:
   - adjacent pairs or triples that match a contiguous n-gram in the Quran;
   - phrases that are semantically offensive.
   For example, keep no two list words that commonly co-occur as a fixed Quranic phrase, or strip such words.
5. **User guidance text** (one short, non-preachy paragraph). It could say:
   - These are ordinary Arabic words, not Quranic verses.
   - Store backups respectfully.
   - When retiring a paper backup, shred it and bury or burn the remains (security and respect align here). For metal backups, grind or scrape off the engraving or melt the plate.
   - Do not leave backups on the floor or in trash.
   - This could be reviewed by the project's Shariah adviser. A one-click "show translation only" mode could be offered.
6. **Shariah review scope.** If seeking a fatwa or certification (e.g., from a gold-token-experienced adviser such as Amanie, or a crypto-focused one such as Amanah Advisors), include the word list, exclusion rules, naming and disposal text in the review packet, alongside the usual financial review.
7. **Multilingual meaning lists.** Translations do not carry the Arabic text's sanctity in most of the sources. One Hanafi answer does treat Quran translations and transliterations as needing respectful disposal — [Askimam 11704](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/11704/is-it-permissible-to-throw-away-papers-with-the-name-of-allah-on-them-or-not-what-is-the-proper-way-to-dispose-of/). That applies to translations *of the Quran*, not to dictionary glosses of single words.

### Gaps
- No source evaluates whether lemma-form vocabulary removes the sanctity concern entirely. That is reasoned from the mushaf-definition and intention rulings, not stated by a scholar.
- No source provides a canonical list of "sacred words" to exclude beyond divine names, angels and prophets. Categories (c) through (h) are a precautionary design inference.

## 6. Would a verbs-only list reduce religious sensitivity compared with nouns?

### Takeaway
Probably yes, on balance (this is inference). The disposal fatwas focus on *names*: Allah's names, angels and messengers. Those are nouns and adjectives, and a verbs-only list automatically excludes nearly all of them. Verbs do not remove all concern, though. Many Quranic verbs describe exclusive divine acts with God as subject (create, give life and death, provide, send down revelation, forgive). Some imperative forms ("qul", "iqra'") are strongly Quranic. Punishment verbs ('adhdhaba, la'ana) could still create offensive pairings. A verbs-only list would still need a sacred-act exclusion pass.

### Cited Findings
- The objects of special disposal care are named as "the name of Allah Ta'ala, His Angels and Messengers", all of them nominal. — [MuftiSays](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/muftisays/10078/how-to-dispose-of-leaflets-and-papers-with-allah-s-name-and-quranic-ayats/); [al-Kawthari](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/daruliftaa/8512/using-a-paper-shredder-to-dispose-of-material-with-quranic-verses-on-them/)
- Divine names exclusive to God include al-Khaliq (Creator), al-Bari', al-Razzaq (Provider) and al-Ahad. The verbs khalaqa, bara'a and razaqa share their roots and meanings with these exclusive names (snippet). — [Islamweb 88517](https://islamweb.net/en/fatwa/88517/using-allaahs-names); [IslamQA 314410](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/314410)
- Intention and function (dhikr vs. recitation; study text vs. money or clothing) govern whether Quranic wording takes Quranic rulings. — [Shafiifiqh](https://islamqa.org/shafii/shafiifiqh/30007/can-one-in-major-ritual-impurity-recite-the-quran-what-about-carry-or-touch-it/)

### Inferences
- Verbs in dictionary form (e.g., kataba "he wrote", dhahaba "he went", akala "he ate") are among the most "ordinary-vocabulary" items in Arabic. Random sequences of past-tense verbs read as a list of actions, not as divine names or scriptural statements. That lowers the chance of juxtaposition problems compared with a mixed noun list.
- A verbs-only list has two non-religious consequences that interact with the religious design:
  - (1) Translations of Arabic verbs into other languages often collapse distinct verb forms (Form I/II/IV) into near-synonyms. The "unique meaning" requirement may therefore force dropping more verbs, and the list may need derived forms that are less familiar.
  - (2) Excluding divine-act verbs, worship verbs (salla, sajada, raka'a, sama, hajja, zakka) and punishment verbs shrinks the pool further.
- Whether enough unique-meaning Quranic verbs remain is a separate lexical question for the word-list researchers.

### Gaps
- No scholar or fatwa was found that compares verbs and nouns for sanctity.
- I did not verify the number of distinct verb lemmas in the Quran in this session. That figure, likely from the Quranic Arabic Corpus, should come from the lexical research track.
